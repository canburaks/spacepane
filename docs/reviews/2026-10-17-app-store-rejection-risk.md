# App Store rejection-risk review — SpacePane 1.1.0 (build 5)

Date: 2026-10-17 (Europe/Istanbul). Scope: the build that is in App Review today, the product page around it, and the surfaces a reviewer opens from it. This is an assessment, not a change: nothing in the repository, in App Store Connect or on the website was modified for it.

## How this was produced

Ten read-only audits ran in parallel, one per dimension (entitlements and manifests; companion completeness; store metadata; privacy; licensing; hidden and dormant code; localization and accessibility; the Quick Look extensions; the website; release records). The lead then verified the findings that could be checked on this Mac without building anything: the kept archives under `.build/archive/`, the live website, and App Store Connect read in the browser pane. Findings that no auditor or the lead could establish are listed as such; nothing below is marked verified on a guess.

Severity means the chance that App Review rejects or stalls on it, weighed with how cheap it is to remove. Blocker: will reject. High: a common rejection pattern that applies as things stand. Medium: plausible objection or a review question that costs a round trip. Low: hygiene.

## Verdict

No blocker was found in the binary, the entitlements, the privacy posture or the licensing. The build is sandboxed, hardened, network-free, manifest-complete and free of dormant CAD code. The risk sits in what the reviewer is told and shown:

1. **Reproduction risk (Guideline 2.1).** The notes send the reviewer to a button in a row that does not contain it, through a System Settings deep link nobody has observed on macOS 26 or 27, and then to an STL sample that Apple's own 3D previewer takes on macOS 27.2. The three 1.1.0 samples the notes describe have never been observed in Finder, and no store- or TestFlight-signed build has ever been installed and exercised.
2. **Metadata accuracy (Guidelines 2.3.1, 2.3.3).** The live description says 74 file types; the build's File Types page says 81. Two of the six live screenshots show a Filter field that build 5 no longer has in Finder previews, and the hero screenshot's Finder list contains `winch-gear.step`, a format the product does not support.
3. **Minimum functionality framing (Guideline 4.2).** The in-app demonstration (Try Preview, Open File) is behind Developer Mode. Without it the companion is settings pages plus the Theme page's live preview. The prepared reply is good; the exposure remains.
4. **Type claims (Quick Look rules, 2.4.5).** The preview extension claims `public.data` (deliberate, disclosed). The thumbnail extension's list is generated from every format whose policy is not `none`, so it also claims folders, app bundles, every archive family, disk images, installer packages, profiles and certificates, and answers them with SpacePane's glyph or the app's icon rather than declining; decision 022 chose this, but no host record shows what Finder does with it, and if Finder asks, Apple's icons are replaced system-wide. Both lists carry 179 `com.quicklookpro.*` identifiers that belong to another developer's product. The first and third are disclosed and defensible; the second needs one observation on a signed host before the next build.

Items 1 and 2 are cheap to fix and expensive to be rejected for. The trade-off the owner must weigh is that fixing the notes, description and screenshots means removing the version from review and resubmitting, which resets the queue position; a rejection also resets it and adds a reply cycle.
